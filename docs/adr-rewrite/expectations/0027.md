## Checklist (most relied-on first)

**C1. What is a trial?** (decision) Cut one problem's instructions down to the
goal behind them (or rewrite a doc or Skill plainly, keeping what it decided),
leave the cut in place for a set time, and judge from what goes wrong
afterwards. Whether a rule matters is found out by removing it, not by reading
it.
Refs 5: explains 3, quotes 2. E.g. `CONTEXT.md:311`, `docs/adr/0003-...md:77`.

**C2. How long does a trial stand, and from when?** (mechanic; home
`scripts/prune-trial-window.ts`, `WINDOW_MS`) About three days, counted from
when the cut lands on `main` (the merge onto main, not when the ledger entry
was written). It is a minimum, not a due date: judging later is fine, judging
earlier is wrong.
Refs 4: enforces 1, explains 1, quotes 1, repeats 1. E.g.
`scripts/prune-trial-window.ts:40`, `.agents/prune-trials.yml:3`.

**C3. What must other sessions do while a trial stands?** (decision) They do
not put back or re-fix the cut text in the trial's area; they log the problem
as a Friction and carry on. Only the `prune-trial` Skill decides the outcome.
A `blocker` Friction may still be filed as an issue naming the trial
(frictions-to-fixes). Before building an issue, check it does not overlap an
open trial.
Refs 4: repeats 2, explains 1, points 1. E.g.
`.agents/skills/frictions-to-fixes/SKILL.md:101`, `docs/agents/issue-tracker.md:31`.

**C4. How is a trial judged?** (decision) The Frictions logged while it stands
are the evidence. Missing evidence never counts as a pass: when the landing
can't be found, the trial is not judged ("silence is not evidence").
Refs 3: explains 1, quotes 1, repeats 1. E.g. `CONTEXT.md:311`,
`scripts/prune-trial-window.ts:93`.

**C5. Where are open trials recorded?** (mechanic; home
`.agents/prune-trials.yml`, checked by `scripts/validate-prune-trials.ts`) One
entry per cut problem, naming the problem and the area (territory) the next
trial must not overlap; the entry ships in the cut's own commit and is deleted
once judged; the file must parse as strict YAML.
Refs 3: explains 2, enforces 1. E.g. `.agents/prune-trials.yml:1`,
`scripts/validate-prune-trials.ts:1`.

**C6. Does a hook have to fail open?** (decision) A hook warns and exits 0,
even when it crashes. Unclear: `prune-trial/SKILL.md:120` applies it to hooks a
trial proposes; `skill-rules-hint.ts` and `stale-info-hint.ts` cite ADR-0027
for it as a rule for every hook. The rewrite must say whether ADR-0027 owns
this for all hooks.
Refs 3: explains 3. E.g. `scripts/skill-rules-hint.ts:6`,
`.agents/skills/prune-trial/SKILL.md:120`.

**C7. Who merges a trial's PR?** (decision) The `prune-trial` run merges its
own PR on a green gate, within its listed scope; a ledger entry never lets an
ADR change skip a human merge.
Refs 3: explains 1, quotes 1, enforces 1. E.g. `docs/adr/0003-...md:24`,
`tests/unit/merge-pr.spec.ts:308`.

**C8. What may a trial change?** (decision) **conflict.** Any agent-instruction
text anywhere except ADRs and their summary rules (`docs/adr/0003-...md:24`,
backed by `tests/unit/merge-pr.spec.ts:308`) — versus ADR-0017, which says it
was "rewritten whole under ADR-0027 with the decision unchanged"
(`docs/adr/0017-...md:7`). The rewrite must say whether ADR-0027 covers
rewriting ADRs outside a trial.
Refs 2: explains 2.

**C9. May a trial write a hook?** (decision) No. When a behaviour proves it
needs a hook, the trial files an issue proposing it, and a session with a
human present builds it.
Refs 2: explains 2. E.g. `docs/adr/0003-...md:24`,
`.agents/skills/prune-trial/SKILL.md:120`.

**C10. May a trial retire a Skill or Routine?** (decision) No; that is filed as
an issue for a human, since no later evidence could show the retirement was a
mistake.
Refs 2: quotes 2. E.g. `docs/adr/0003-...md:77`.

**C11. What do you do when one of our instructions causes a problem?**
(decision) Cut or simplify that instruction before adding a new one.
Refs 1: explains 1. `CLAUDE.md:8`.
