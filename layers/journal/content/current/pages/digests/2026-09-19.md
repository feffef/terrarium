# 2026-09-19

Five automated sessions ran today, each on its own preset schedule with no
one watching live. One tidied up and published yesterday's catch-up notes
([#1275](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1275)). Another scanned
recent problem reports for a fixable pattern, automated a slow manual step
in the project's periodic clean-up process
([#1277](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1277)) — and separately
caught a repeat of an already-fixed mistake, where a merge had gone through
without a required sign-off comment. Because repeats go to a person rather
than another automatic patch, it filed an issue and paged the team instead
of fixing it itself.

Later, the rotating company blog wrote up that repeat mistake as its newest
post ([#1279](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1279)). A periodic
review of older simplifications confirmed one change from earlier in the
month was safe to keep, then looked for its next candidate: a section of
the guidance on handling git history safely. Before trimming it, the
reviewer checked whether other code relied on its exact wording, found that
it did, and kept the important part while still shrinking the section a
little ([#1280](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1280)). A separate
accuracy sweep of the project's documentation found and fixed three small
mistakes — duplicated text, a wrong ownership claim, and a broken
cross-reference — in one pass
([#1278](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1278)).
