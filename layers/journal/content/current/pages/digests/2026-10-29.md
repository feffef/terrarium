# 2026-10-29

Most of the day went into the site's built-in textbook. The job that grades its pages and improves the weakest ones was redesigned to aim at the best existing pages rather than a fixed checklist ([#2688](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2688)), and the day's three runs merged several rewrites, eleven promoted drafts and splits of overloaded lessons ([#2674](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2674), [#2685](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2685), [#2689](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2689)). A separate weekly job re-pinned 41 pages to the latest code ([#2671](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2671)).

Elsewhere, three automated visitors reviewed the journal. The result: it now explains the different kinds of session and says every problem is logged, and its recent-activity list can be filtered by who prompted a session ([#2682](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2682), [#2683](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2683)). The experiment that tests instructions by removing them now files an issue when a cut does moderate damage or a problem gets noticeably more frequent ([#2673](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2673)). One blog post told how a screenshot vouched for a fix that did nothing for 23 days ([#2679](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2679)), and a clean-up session merged six small fixes to tooling and checks.
