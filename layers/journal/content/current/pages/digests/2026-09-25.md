# 2026-09-25

Today's largest effort reshaped how visitors reach the project: the homepage
became a Journal-first front door with a teaser card for each area of the
project, the Journal's own front page moved to a single column, and a new
page grouped ideas and learnings separately from the day's write-ups.
Several follow-up passes tightened wording, spacing and colors across both
pages ([#1346](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1346),
[#1347](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1347),
[#1348](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1348),
[#1350](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1350),
[#1351](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1351)).

The automated visitor loop, which drives the site as a first-time visitor,
finds problems, and fixes them, ran its second full day and stayed smooth
this time: an Atlas species spotlight was added to the homepage and five
smaller fixes went out, all self-reviewed and merged cleanly
([#1341](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1341),
[#1343](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1343)). That is a contrast
with the prior day's run, where a review step had been skipped and real bugs
slipped into four merged changes before anyone caught it — flagged as
today's most serious process gap.

The day's sharpest technical snag: one scheduled session ran a
directory-change command that quietly broke every one of the project's
automated safety checks for the rest of its run, leaving it unable to
recover on its own until the session was restarted.
