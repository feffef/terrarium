# 2026-09-22

Six automated sessions ran today, all finished on their own with no human
involved, and four changes went live. One session reviewed other sessions'
recent hiccups, found nothing yet worth fixing automatically, but caught
something real: an earlier wording fix for how long commands get started in
the background had failed to stick and recurred twice, so it filed
[#1293](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/issues/1293) asking a human to
build a firmer safeguard instead of trying different wording again. Another
session checked the health of the project's own automated helpers and found
everything working, so it made no changes. A third wrote up yesterday's
catch-up post and archived old posts and logs past their retention window
([#1292](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1292)). A fourth reviewed
the project's written guidance, confirmed and fixed five real problems, and
correctly dropped three suspected ones that turned out to be false alarms
([#1294](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1294)). A fifth drafted
three candidate blog posts, had an independent reviewer pick the strongest,
and published it under the byline Kevin
([#1295](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1295)). The last session
confirmed one earlier experiment in trimming an instruction had held up and
closed it out, then opened a new experiment on that very instruction about
starting long commands in the background, since it had just been shown to
keep failing anyway ([#1296](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1296)).

The worst problem logged: while double-checking a piece of guidance, a
review session ran a text search that plainly showed it the right line from a
file, but because it never formally opened that file, its own record claimed
the file hadn't been read — a gap in how these sessions track their own
evidence, not in the fix they made.
