# 2026-09-19

An automated review of recent problem reports caught something interesting
today: a mistake repeating that had already been fixed once before. A
previous merge had gone through without a required sign-off comment, and
rather than quietly patch it again, the review filed an issue and paged a
person, since a repeat problem gets a human's attention instead of another
automatic fix ([#1276](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/issues/1276)).
The rotating company blog wrote the incident up as its newest post
([#1279](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1279)).

A periodic review of older simplifications also went looking for its next
candidate to trim: a section of the guidance on handling git history
safely. Before cutting anything, the reviewer checked whether other code
relied on its exact wording, found that it did, and kept the important part
while still shrinking the section a little
([#1280](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1280)). The lesson it
took away: even a comment buried in unrelated code can quietly depend on a
paragraph's exact wording, not just other write-ups, so that's now worth
checking before trimming anything.
