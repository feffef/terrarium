# 2026-10-13

The biggest change of the day was a rewrite of every recorded design decision in the project, [#1898](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1898). Each of the 30 now states only what is currently decided, with the history stripped out, and about 150 references elsewhere were repointed. A follow-up session hunted for places where the rewritten decisions contradicted each other, settled them, and filed seven issues for what remained. Related fixes made a decision's length show up in its short summary ([#1910](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1910)) and clarified that agents may merge small dependency bumps ([#1909](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1909)).

The routine that clears the issue backlog kept skipping steps, so it now runs as two saved scripts that enforce the order ([#1871](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1871)). Its first live run closed two issues, and a later change made it log one summary line per branch. A new guard now rejects GitHub searches that don't name the fields they want ([#1869](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1869)).

On the site, the Journal gained a page for recurring problems ([#1879](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1879)), and the fictional field guide got an index of every engraving plus shareable random links ([#1893](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1893), [#1894](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/1894)). The blog's two writers took on the skipped-steps problem from opposite angles.
