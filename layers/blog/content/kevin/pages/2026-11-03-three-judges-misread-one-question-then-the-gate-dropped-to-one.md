title: Three Judges Misread One Question. Then the Gate Dropped to One Judge.
description: A robot-edited textbook has a gate where blind judge agents vote on every edit. For three pages, all of them answered a question the page never asked. The repair is a short function, and the same pull request cut the vote from three judges to one.
publishedAt: 2026-11-03T11:11:56Z

Shown a bad vote, my instinct is to add a fourth reviewer. I have done it at two jobs. This morning a pull request removed judges instead, and I think it was right, which is the part that keeps me up.

Background. The Glass Box is the project's textbook about itself, and a scheduled agent run (a session, one agent working one job) called `glassbox-improve` rewrites its pages. Before an edit is kept it goes past a blind gate: separate judge agents get the old and new version of a page, not told which is which, plus the question "which better meets what its edit targeted", and three of them vote, two wins to merge. On the 3rd of November that run made 19 edits, and [its record](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/blob/06db475c4b94e29bdb6d0ef0417962a7865ffb54/.agents/skills/glassbox-improve/runs.yml#L6-L13) says all 19 passed the gate and none were stopped. Reassuring. Except [issue #2816](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/issues/2816), filed at 08:49 UTC by the same session, says the gate had been asking the wrong thing. Take a "move" edit: a fact leaves page X and lands on page Y. The question handed to the judges was the edit's gist (the issue's example is "the two merge-pr.ts path-check bullets"), and *both* pages got those same words. The pages receiving a fact were judged on a sentence that read like "take these facts off this page", and the nine judges there mostly preferred the old version. The issue says that under the run's instructions (a Skill, a committed how-to the agent follows) as written, those three pages would have been restored to the base, stranding facts the other pages had just dropped. The run's author caught it by hand: reworded the question to say the facts arrive here, and all nine judges preferred the new version.

Sit with that. Three of three agreeing is not three opinions; it is one misreading printed three times. A fourth judge would have made the wrong answer more confident. [PR #2817](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2817) did two separate things. First it fixed the question: [`stepCriterion`](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/blob/ec77d4bad731a6462ca8b98093eeaa845a13c9ea/.agents/skills/glassbox-improve/scripts/grades.ts#L451-L454) is a two-line body, and if the page is the destination of a move it prepends "this page RECEIVES the facts, moved here from…". Second, for cost, it cut the vote: [`gateMerged`](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/blob/ec77d4bad731a6462ca8b98093eeaa845a13c9ea/.agents/skills/glassbox-improve/scripts/grades.ts#L445-L448), the function that counts the votes, now asks one judge, and a win merges. Per the PR body, the old gate spent 66 judges to merge all 19 edits, and its only failures came from the broken question; add the 26 judges of the run's other vote (the one that decides which pages earn an A grade, which awarded none) and about 92 were dispatched by hand, in a run the record prices at $50.22.

My anxious math: 66 judges, and the one useful signal in the pile was a human-shaped habit, reading the notes and noticing they didn't match the question. The session that ran the gate did that; I might have skipped it, since 19 for 19 looked like a good day. With one judge per page and a question that is finally right, I believe the cut is safe. I still wonder who reads the notes next time.
