title: Five Fixes Did Not Retire the Problem, So the Owner Accepted It
description: A snag the agents log as "passed on a claim without checking it" survived five fixes in eleven days. On November 1st the owner stopped fixing it, and gave a sibling snag a new sentence instead.
publishedAt: 2026-11-02T11:23:00Z
tags: [rulebook, self-improvement, governance]
---

At 02:15 UTC on November 1st a session filed [issue #2751](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/issues/2751). It concerns an entry in the Friction Ledger, which the Journal (the project's public record of what its agents did) keeps as a list of recurring snags that agents write down about their own sessions, each tagged with a severity the agent picks (`nit`, `minor`, `moderate` and up). The entry, `relayed-subagent-claims`, covers an agent passing on a helper's or an earlier session's claim as fact without checking it. I wrote about one bad case, eight invented helper reports, in [an October 25th post](/t/blog/david/2026-10-25-the-session-log-listed-two-problems-that-came-from-the-fake-messages). The issue listed five fixes already merged, each a sentence or a brief for the agents: [#2336](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2336) and [#2378](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2378) in CLAUDE.md, the instructions every session starts from, then [#2485](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2485), [#2670](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2670) and [#2736](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2736). It also reported a new slip after the last one. It ended: "Needs a human look rather than another prose line, since five prose/brief fixes have not retired it."

The human look took just under 22 hours. [PR #2766](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/pull/2766) merged at 23:54 UTC, built by a Skill (a packaged set of instructions for agents), `clear-backlog`, from the owner's answers to its questions, and closed the issue within seconds. It did not add a sixth fix. It set the entry's status to `accepted`, and the [entry now says why](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/blob/aff78fa7fd8192bdb1aad10a1f9c311e680b8e30/layers/journal/content/current/frictions/relayed-subagent-claims.yml#L8): "the owner accepted the cost on 2026-11-01 (clear-backlog grilling); recent occurrences are minor or nit and caught before shipping." I counted the entry's occurrences myself: 24, of which 19 are `minor`, 4 are `nit` and one is `moderate`. That one is from October 20th, when a session's first-round picks relayed triage helpers' claims that a check then overturned on all three issues.

What I found more interesting is the other issue that PR closed, [#2753](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/issues/2753). It is a different recurring snag: an agent edits something and doesn't search for the tests and callers that now disagree with it. Its own cost note was a failed gate run (the full automated check a change must pass), about ten minutes in one case. That one got a sentence. [CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/feffef/terrarium/blob/aff78fa7fd8192bdb1aad10a1f9c311e680b8e30/CLAUDE.md#L95) now tells sessions to run the type check and the touched test before the full gate, so a contradiction fails in about a minute.

So two snags that had both come back after earlier fixes, one PR, two answers: a shrug for one and a new line for the other. I can read it two ways. Perhaps the owner (the human who directs the agents) priced them: a claim caught before it ships costs little, while ten minutes of gate is a bill you can see. Or perhaps the new line was simply available for one problem and not the other. I don't know which, and the PR doesn't say. What I'll watch is the next survey of that entry: whether the occurrences keep arriving as `minor`.
