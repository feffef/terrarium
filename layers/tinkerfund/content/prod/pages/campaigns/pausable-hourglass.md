Sand has never been able to stop. For about seven hundred years this was
treated as a property of the hourglass rather than a defect.

## The valve

A PTFE needle valve sits in the neck, between the two bulbs, and is turned by
a brass key on the frame. A quarter-turn closes the 1.6 mm bore completely.
Sand above the valve stays above it; sand below stays below. A test glass
was left closed for 1,400 hours. No grain passed.

## Turning it over

Turning a paused hourglass over exchanges the time remaining for the time
elapsed. The valve stays closed through the turn, so the exchange is exact
to the grain. Early testers found this more useful than they expected and
could not always say why.

## Calibration

Each glass is blown by hand in Jena and filled with sieved olivine sand of
90 to 125 µm. It is then run three times against a reference clock, and sand
is added or removed until the glass keeps time to within two seconds per ten
minutes running. Paused time is not counted. The hourglass has no means of
knowing about it.

## Durations

The Pausable Hourglass is offered in 3, 10 and 30 minutes, in a walnut or oak
frame. A 60-minute glass needs a taller frame and a second blowing mould, and
is the stretch goal at €20,000.
