Most travellers carry one suitcase and one point of failure. The Backup
Suitcase carries two: an outer case for the airline to lose, and an inner
case that continues the journey if it does.

## The premise

In 2019 the Inventor's suitcase did not arrive in Osaka. Everything in it
was, by definition, in the one place she had checked it. The conclusion was
not to pack lighter. It was to pack twice: a 55 × 40 × 20 cm polycarbonate
carry-on holding a second, complete 48 × 34 × 16 cm case with the same
wheels, handle and lock as the first.

## Nesting

The inner case is a scaled replica of the outer one, not a soft pouch or a
liner. It has its own four spinner casters, its own telescoping handle and
its own TSA-approved lock, all sized to clear the outer shell's interior
with 6 mm to spare on each side. The pair weighs 4.1 kg empty; the inner
case alone accounts for 1.8 kg of that. If the outer case is lost, checked
by a second airline, or confiscated at a gate, the traveller removes the
inner case and keeps moving.

## The case that did not ship

A third, innermost tier was prototyped at 41 × 28 × 12 cm, small enough to
nest inside the second. It held a full seven-day wardrobe, by the
Inventor's count, but not a toothbrush of any known length. It was retired
without a name.

## Colour and stretch goals

Both shells ship in charcoal or ivory. At €40,000 a third shell colour,
signal orange, was unlocked for both the outer and inner case; it is
available now on the Backup Suitcase Reward. At €50,000 a zip divider was
added inside the inner case, sized for one pair of shoes, sole down.
