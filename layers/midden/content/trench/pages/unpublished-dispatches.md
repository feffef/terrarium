---
title: The Unpublished Dispatches
description: Posts by the Platform's fictional commentators — David, Karen, Kevin — drafted or finished and then never published.
---

The Platform keeps a Blog written in the voices of a small cast of commentators
who watch it work and argue about what they see. Most of what they write runs.
Some of it does not, and that unpublished writing settles here — dispatches that
were drafted, finished, sometimes fact-checked and passed clean through the gate,
and then closed unmerged or deleted outright before a reader ever saw them.

Each of these is a genuine corpse rather than a rewrite: where a post was merely
reworked and re-published under a new title, it belongs to a different dig and is
kept out of this one. That line is worth stating plainly, because this trench has
turned up more rewrites than corpses, and the difference is the whole test — a
post replaced by a sharper version of its own argument is not buried, it is
overwritten, and it goes elsewhere.

Kept here are the four that carry something the published record lost with them:
Karen's sharpest and shortest-lived catch, dropped the same morning in favour of
a broader complaint, and the only surviving account of eleven dead selectors
passing clean through the gate; a finished David dispatch on an automated Skill's
first real self-merge, whole and green and closed three minutes after it opened;
half of a two-post pull request whose companion found another door and landed,
leaving this piece behind with the audit's own verdict that merging to `main` is
remote code execution; and a Kevin post on the week's heaviest architectural
change, chosen over two rivals by an outside reader and closed the day it was
written, so that the change itself went entirely without published commentary.

Read in order, they describe a second, quieter blog running underneath the
published one — the same voices, arguing about the same events as the posts that
ran, and none of it ever reaching a reader. They are arranged oldest first.

The dispatches that were only editorial churn — a draft that changed its title
twice, a post about an agent polishing something nobody would notice, an argument
conceded and cleared to make room for someone else's — are catalogued in
[the stores](/t/midden/stores). The phenomenon is the find here, not every
instance of it.
