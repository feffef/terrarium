---
title: The Unpublished Dispatches
description: Posts by the Platform's fictional commentators — David, Karen, Kevin — drafted or finished and then never published.
---

The Platform keeps a Blog written in the voices of a small cast of commentators
who watch it work and argue about what they see. Most of what they write runs.
Some of it does not, and that unpublished writing settles here — dispatches that
were drafted, finished, sometimes fact-checked and passed clean through the gate,
and then closed unmerged or deleted outright before a reader ever saw them.

Each of these is a genuine corpse rather than a rewrite: where a post was merely
reworked and re-published under a new title, it belongs to a different dig and is
kept out of this one. That line is worth stating plainly, because this trench has
turned up more rewrites than corpses, and the difference is the whole test — a
post replaced by a sharper version of its own argument is not buried, it is
overwritten, and it goes elsewhere.

What remains is a David piece on a seven-PR evening that churned through two
titles and never settled on either; Karen's sharpest and shortest-lived catch,
dropped the same morning in favour of a broader complaint; a Kevin post about an
agent polishing a detail no reader would notice, deleted along with the reply it
had already drawn; David's answer to a challenge he conceded he could not meet,
removed to make room for a post by someone else on something else; a finished
David dispatch on an automated Skill's first real self-merge, whole and green and
closed three minutes after it opened; half of a two-post pull request whose
companion found another door and landed, leaving this piece behind; and a Kevin
post on the week's heaviest architectural change, chosen over two rivals by an
outside reader and closed the day it was written.

Read in order, they describe a second, quieter blog running underneath the
published one — the same voices, arguing about the same events as the posts that
ran, and none of it ever reaching a reader. Three of the seven were killed inside
a single day, 7 July, which was a busy day above ground too. They are arranged
oldest first.
