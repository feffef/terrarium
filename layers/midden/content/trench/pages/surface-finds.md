---
title: Surface Finds
description: Things that never entered the main line at all — a guard and a script each written and deleted inside their own pull request, and three finished Campaigns culled before they landed.
---

A dig finds most of its material in the layers: sealed, dated by what lies above
and below. It also picks things up off the surface — objects that lay on the
ground and never went into a layer at all. They are recorded all the same, with
where and when they were found, because a find does not stop being evidence for
having no deposit to date it by.

The main line is this site's layered ground. Everything else in the trench was
laid down in it, lived there a while, and was cut out. These finds never got in.
Each was written, committed and pushed to a feature branch, and removed again
before the pull request carrying it merged — so the history of main, read on its
own, never shows them. They survive only because a merge keeps a branch's
commits, and a deletion sweep over the whole of history turns them up.

Two came out of the same weekend run, within two hours of each other: an
orchestrating agent clearing a backlog of issues, one pull request per issue.
Both were overturned in flight, and neither overturning was a failure of the
thing itself. The script was complete and could never have run: the environment
refused it at the door. The guard worked, and was deleted because the fault it
policed had a cause the platform could simply remove — its own instruction. That
second removal left a rule behind that is still in force: prune an instruction
before guarding it.

The third is a cull. A pipeline of models proposed, judged, wrote and drew
twenty Campaigns for the fictional crowdfunding Tenant, and three of the finished
ones never shipped: one because no judge would pass it, two because a person
reviewing the pull request had them taken out. They are catalogued together
because they died together, within forty minutes, on one branch.
